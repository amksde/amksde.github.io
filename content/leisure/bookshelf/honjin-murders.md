### Review
Got literal nostalgia on reading Kindaichi's name. Had a bit trouble with the amount of Japanese words that were used throughout the book, had to constantly look up what they meant. The final explanation was good, but the murder trick felt underwhelming.

### Rating
7 / 10

### Summary
A creepy man starts asking around for Ichiyanagi family house, and two days later, the house's eldest son and his bride are killed on their wedding night.

The bride's uncle calls upon a young detective, Kosuke Kindaichi. There's not much to the plot apart from a severe let down at the end when the mechanism of the murder is explained. The motive in itself is pretty dark, but the execution was something I can only describe as "Ocean's Eleven" theatrics in a Japanese mystery setting.

In short, the eldest son had killed the bride and then committed suicide. His motive being possible shame being brought upon the family by the bride's ex-boyfriend (about whom he got to know just a few days before the marriage).

With the help of his psychotic younger brother, he arranged a mechanical system that would allow the weapon to be thrown out of the house after the murder had been committed.
