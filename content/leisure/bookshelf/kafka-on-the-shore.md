### Summary

The story takes place in the early 2000s. Kafka Tamura runs away from his home on his 15th birthday. He gets on a bus and leaves for a town called Takamatsu. He alludes to an "omen" that his father told him about. On the way, he befriends a 21-year-old girl named Sakura who is going the same way.

We are also told about an incident that took place in 1944 in Japan, where a group of schoolchildren aged 9 to 10 years suddenly collapse on a mushroom gathering trip on a nearby mountainside. The reason is never really conclusively revealed. One of the leading investigators from the Japanese side (Americans investigated this incident as well) believes this to be a case of mass hypnosis of some sort. All the children in the group eventually regain consciousness within a few hours, except a boy named Nakata. He wakes a few weeks later with complete memory loss and impaired cognitive functioning.

In the present day, we are reintroduced to Nakata, who is now an old man. He lives a quiet life on a disability pension from the government, while also working as a professional cat-finder. An ability to talk to cats, which he gained after the aforementioned incident, helps him make a side income as a cat-finder. At the time, he was given the task to find a cat called Goma.

Kafka arrives in Takamatsu, and while exploring the city, reaches a quiet, secluded library named Komura Memorial Library. There he befriends the receptionist, Oshima. He lives in a hotel, works out at the gym, and spends most of the day reading at the library. But one day, while returning to the hotel, he suddenly blacks out and wakes up in a bush near a shrine with blood on his clothes. He panics and calls the only person he knows in the town, Sakura. She allows him to stay at her house, but they also have a very brief non-penetrative sexual encounter there.

The next morning, he leaves Sakura's place and visits the library again, where Oshima realizes he is in peril and offers to set him up to live in a spare room in the library and work there as his assistant.

Meanwhile, Nakata finds Goma at the residence of a mysterious man who calls himself Johnny Walker. He claims he kills cats, beheads them, and then collects their souls to make a certain kind of flute. He says he *has* to do this, he *cannot* stop, though he is tired of it. He asks Nakata to kill him and set him free. Though initially reluctant, when Nakata sees him kill some of the cats he knew in front of him, something overtakes his spirit *and* body, and stabs Johnny Walker to death. Surprisingly, he has no blood splatter on him. The next day, though, he starts travelling west (towards Takamatsu eventually) because it is something *he has to do*.

Kafka, meanwhile, gets to know about the caretaker of the library, Miss Saeki. She was the lover of the eldest son of the Komura family, who had set up this library in a section of his house and used to live there alone. Miss Saeki, at that time, was studying piano music, and had composed a famous song called ***Kafka On The Shore***. This song expressed her longing for her lover, who had gone away to Tokyo for further studies. During some violent clashes at his university, he was killed. Miss Saeki, distraught, left everything and ran away from home. For around twenty-five years, no one knew where she was. One day, she suddenly showed up, and the Komura family put her in charge of the library and its grounds.

The timeline suggests that Miss Saeki might have been Kafka's long-lost mother who had left him and his father and taken his sister with her. But at the same time, he feels romantically (and sexually) attracted to her. Kafka begins seeing the younger Miss Saeki's spirit in his room at the library. The spirit used to come to his room around 3 a.m. every night and used to look at a painting of Kafka Kamura from his younger days. Miss Saeki and Kafka (Tamura) eventually sleep together and get romantically involved.

Nakata, on the other hand, is on his trip to Takamatsu. He befriends a lorry driver named Hoshino who accompanies him to the city, considering this his long-deserved vacation. At the same time, police have started looking for him in connection to Johnny Walker's murder, who, as it turns out, was Kafka Tamura's father. The police start looking for Kafka as well. Kafka also lets us know about the "omen" - that pushed him to leave his house. In an Oedipal phrophecy, his father had told him that Kafka would eventually kill him and sleep with his mother and sister. 

Nakata seems to have visions and dreams about things he *has to do*. He asks Hoshino to look for a stone in Takamatsu, which is some sort of a portal to another world. When the portal is opened, Nakata visits Saeki and lets her know that her time had come, and she must leave this world now. At the same time, Oshima has Kafka stay at his forest cabin to hide him from police. Kafka goes wandering in the forest and stumbles upon an entrance into the other world as well. He meets Miss Saeki there who convinces him to go back to the real world.

After this episode, Nakata dies and Hoshino closes the entrance via the stone. Kafka realizes he has fulfilled his father's prophecy somehow - killing him in a manner which he doesn't remember, and has slept with his mother (Miss Saeki) and his sister (Sakura) - neither blood relation actually confirmed in the book. Kafka plans to return to Tokyo and complete high school.

The end.

### Review

The plot was utter bullshit. The story is definitely a page-turner. You get questions after every chapter you read. The vocabulary and the setting of the story give it a very slow and comforting essence.

Although we had many questions, none of them were answered by the end. Was Miss Saeki actually Kafka's mother? If yes, then what happened to Kafka's sister? Sakura's family history makes it unlikely that she is his sister. Maybe the curse wasn't meant to come to fruition in a literal sense. Just the fact that Kafka saw Sakura as his sister (which feels *very* weird).

The story of the "entrance" and its relation to Johnny Walker wasn't expanded at all. Was this man connected to the eel that came out of Nakata's corpse's mouth? Was Johnny Walker actually Koichi Tamura? It honestly felt a colossal waste of time. Like I said, the story was definitely a page-turner, but the story led nowhere. But then again, I am not really a big fan of inconclusive stories. I feel they represent a lack of decision-making on the part of the author. Some might call it art which is open to interpretation.

That being said, I actually loved the characters of Oshima and Hoshino. They felt very raw and real at the same time. The way these two went out of their way to help their respective friends was awesome.

The pacing was almost perfect. It could very well have been a script of some fantasy anime. It literally felt very similar to Your Name or Spirited Away, in keeping with the theme of Japanese stories around unexplained spirits.

I enjoyed reading the book, but not the ending. Not at all. Not recommended at all.
