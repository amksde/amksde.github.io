### Rating
8 / 10

### Review
Awesome book. A classic whodunnit. But, impossible to make this into a movie. When I reached the end, I thought it had started to resemble "And Then There Were None", and frankly, some of the people were making the mistakes that I found were pretty dumb given they were a mystery club. Apart from one instance, the 'decagon' shape did not really have much significance for the plot. It could have been a simple house as well, and nothing would change. But a good read nonetheless.

### Summary
A mystery club goes on a trip to an unhabited island for a week to stay in decagon house made by an a famous dead architect Nakamura Seiji. The members include Ellery, Poe, Agatha, Orczy, Van, Carr, and Leroux. Unbeknownst to them, Seiji was the father of Chiori, a member of their club who had died earlier during a club after-party due to alcohol poisoning.

On the mainland, we have an ex-member of the club, Kawanimani, and a current member, Morisu, receive letters from Seiji accusing the club members of killing her daughter.

The novel continously shifts the point-of-view between the island and the mainland. This is done to establish the alibi of Morisu, who is in fact Van. He has decided to deliver judgement to the rest of the club members for their crime of killing Chiori, with whom he was in love with.

The tension in the house on the island feels very real. The residents continously juggle between the idea of the murderer being an outsider or someone in their group, while actually both are kind of the case. The author plays between the two perspectives. As a reader, I had a lot of suspicons. The murderer could be either

- Poe (experiences from 'And Then There Were None')
- Leroux (experieces from 'ABC Murders')
- and ofc, Van (cuz common sense!)
