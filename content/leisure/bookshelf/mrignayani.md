- **Genre** : Historical Fiction
- **Author** : Vrindavanlal Verma
- **Year** : 1950

### Summary
The story starts with 3 main characters - Mrignayani (nicknamed Ninni), Laakhi Raani (Ninni's close friend), and Atal Singh (Ninni's brother), in a village named Rai near Gwalior. Ninni and Laakhi routinely go for hunting, predominantly a male activity in their area, and are often able to successfully kill tigers, water buffaloes, wild pigs, etc. The village priest Bodhan goes to Gwalior and brags about them to the king, Raja Man Singh Tomar, who, being pleasantly surprised, promises to visit the village and see them in action.

The tale about the girls' beauty and valor spreads beyond Gwalior as well to Ghiyasuddin Khilji in Malwa as well. His close aid sends a group of nomads to their village to rope them in somehow and bring them to Ghiyas' court / harem. In one of their initial trials, they sneak in four of Ghiyas' soldiers to capture them during a hunt. The nomads had sent them with a false lead to a secluded place where the girls encountered the said soldiers. During the confrontation, the girls manage to kill 2 of them and scare away the other 2 (who are eventually killed by Ghiyas).

Hearing about this incident, Man Singh visits Rai village a symbol of security to assure the villagers. He is smitten by Mrignayani there as soon as he sees him. The girls and Atal join Man Singh during a hunting game, and when Man Singh sees that Ninni kills a tiger with a single arrow and topples a water buffalo with her bare hands, he confesses here love and asks her hand in marriage. Though they are of different castes (which is a problem at that time as well), they get married and face no objections. Ninni goes off to Gwalior palace after the marriage.

Atal had liked Laakhi for some time now, and had also promised to marry her. The village priest Bodhan and the villages objected to their marriage since both of them belonged to different castes (Atal was Gujar, and Laakhi was Ahir). They had earlier assumed that the inter-case marriage of Man Singh and Ninni would help them convince the villagers, but alas, that did not happen. The got married in a Gandharva Vivah like ritual. The nomads sent by Ghiyas used their fear of persecution by the villagers to elope with them to a nearby village near Narvar fort. As it happened, Ghiyas had also started a war campaign against Gwalior to get to the two girls, and had reached near Narvar as well. News of Ghiyas' campaign reached Man Singh as well, and he marched towards Narvar as well.

The nomads, along with Atal and Laakhi, sheltered themselves initially inside the Narvar fort. Two members of their troop returned with a plan to escape to Ghiyas using a rope to escape, and using the same rope, let his army inside the fort (which was practically impenetrable). The niece of the troop's leader, Pilli, eventually confessed to Laakhi that Ghiyas had sent them for her, and she would be like a queen with him. Moreover, she told her that she wanted Atal for herself, and that all of them would be happy in Malwa (sick, right?). Laakhi deceived them at the end, killing Pilli, telling Atal everything, and alerted the guards at the fort. She got praised for her bravery, and when recognized by Man Singh (who had arrived with his army just in time), was taken to Gwalior with Atal.

They lived happily for some time. The eldest queen disliked both Ninni and Laakhi since they were from a lower caste, and tried to poison them on multiple occasions, but never succeeded. In Malwa, Ghiyas is poisoned by his son Naseer, who focuses his entire energy to create a harem with fifteen thousand women. In Agra, Bodhan is killed by Sikandar Lodhi after he declared in a debate that Islam and Hinduism are but two ways to the same end goal, and that he wouldn't convert to Islam

The story then describes how Baiju Bawra became popular, creation of Sitar (simpler instrument than Veena), and how Man Singh was adamant on popularizing art and music in his regime. Some mentions include creating Gujari raag - attributed in this novel to have been made with the help of Gujari queen, Mrignayani. It also describes the construction of Maan Mandir, and a special palace - Gujari Mahal - that Man Singh had made for Ninni. This palace had supply of water from Saank river from the Rai village, which Ninni had asked as a promise in return for agreeing to marry him. Repeated attacks by Sikandar Lodhi test their resolve to balance art and wars at the same time. 

During one such battle with Sikandar Lodhi, Laakhi and then Atal sacrificed their lives protecting the villagers in the fort that Man Singh had constructed on the border of Rai village. It was a truly emotional to be honest. When Laakhi was dying, she asked Atal to promise him that he will remarry. Just a few hours later, when Atal died, he told he had kept his promise of remarrying, but that his second marriage will be with Laakhi only, just in another world.

The novel ends with Man Singh and Ninni watching one of her incomplete paintings showing balance of duty and art in Man Singh's regime. Laakhi's necklace hangs in the corner where duty was depicted.
